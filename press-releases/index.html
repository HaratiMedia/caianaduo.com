🎵 Caiana Duo Releases "Bebê" — A Baião Tribute to Hermeto Pascoal

Published: September 18, 2026
Location: Munich, Germany

Brazilian instrumental group Caiana Duo — featuring 7-string nylon guitar player Abdallah Harati and pandeiro specialist João Araújo — releases Bebê, a Baião interpretation of a composition by the legendary Brazilian composer and multi-instrumentalist Hermeto Pascoal.

Continuing the duo's approach of bringing lesser-known corners of the Brazilian songbook to new audiences, Bebê channels Hermeto Pascoal's playful, genre-defying spirit through the rhythmic drive of Baião — a style rooted in Brazil's Northeast.

🌿 About Caiana Duo

Caiana Duo is a Brazilian instrumental music project formed by Abdallah Harati (violão 7 cordas) and João Araújo (pandeiro). Their sound blends Choro, Samba, Capoeira, and other Afro-Brazilian rhythms into intimate, dynamic acoustic performances. Drawing on real instruments and deep tradition, their arrangements are spontaneous, heartfelt, and rooted in the essence of Brazilian culture.

The name Caiana refers to a resilient Afro-Brazilian plant — symbolic of healing, rhythm, and grounded energy — echoing the duo's musical philosophy.

🎸 About Abdallah Harati

Abdallah Harati is a Brazilian-German guitarist, composer, and producer known for his mastery of the violão 7 cordas (seven-string nylon guitar). A key figure in the Brazilian jazz and Choro scenes in Europe, he bridges tradition and innovation through self-produced recordings, festival direction, and collaborations with musicians across genres. He is also the founder of Harati Media and co-leads Caiana Duo.

🎵 Caiana Duo Releases "Viajeiro" — A Stripped-Down Homage to Brazilian Jazz, Choro, and Sebastião Tapajós

Published: May 30, 2025
Location: Munich, Germany / São Paulo, Brazil

Brazilian instrumental group Caiana Duo — featuring 7-string nylon guitar player Abdallah Harati and pandeiro specialist João Araújo — has released a new single, Viajeiro, a soulful tribute to legendary composer Sebastião Tapajós.

Rooted in the deep tradition of Choro, Chorinho, and Brazilian Jazz, Viajeiro revives a nearly forgotten gem.

"I've known this tune for years and always found it hauntingly beautiful," says Harati. "When I played it for João for the first time, he was immediately moved. We knew we had to give it a voice again."

The duo recorded Viajeiro in studio using only violão de 7 cordas (seven-string nylon guitar) and pandeiro, capturing a raw, elegant dialogue between melody and rhythm. The result is an intimate conversation that blends tradition, emotion, and spontaneity — a tribute to acoustic Brazilian music at its finest.

"We didn't want to modernize it — we wanted to respect the tune with the raw tools of our tradition," Harati adds.
